Why it scores that way

The friction weighting

Recording something that went wrong is worth 20 — more than naming a project, more than naming who you dealt with. It is the heaviest thing on the sheet apart from logging at all.

That is deliberate. The reflective boxes are what Part 3 examiners actually read, and the most-cited weakness in them is describing rather than reflecting. A week where everything went smoothly gives you nothing to write, so it caps at 80 here.

Two rules the code enforces

It never invents hours

No stated duration means zero, not a guess. Filling blanks is a separate, explicit action that marks every minute it estimated — a mentor signs these records, and a plausible fabricated number is worse than an obvious blank.

The optional model pass may improve wording and tagging. It is not allowed to set hours, and it may only pick projects, stages and criteria that exist. Everything else is discarded in code, not discouraged in a prompt.

The finding that reshaped the app

The two-month deadline

A record sheet covers up to three months and must be completed within two months of that period ending. Miss it and the sheet is late: your PSA can no longer give you useful feedback, and a run of late sheets is a number an examiner can count against your time management.

It is buried in guidance PDFs and no tool surfaces it. The app counts down to every one.

Scope This page is the parser only. The full app adds the record itself, the register, coverage across RIBA stages 0–7 and PC1–PC5, the deadline engine, generated quarterly sheets, and a calendar feed that reminds you. Nothing you type here is stored or sent. It is not the official record either — that is RIBA's system at pedr.co.uk, where your mentor and PSA sign.
